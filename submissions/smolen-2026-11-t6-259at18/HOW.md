# How this array was built

Construction: an array invariant under a column group, Z17 with a multiplier subgroup of order 8 and one fixed
column, made of 7 word orbits and 4 constant rows, with the words found by an exact SAT model.

CA(259; 6, 18, 2).

1. The group. Columns 0 to 16 are the residues modulo 17. The group G generated by x -> x + 1 and x -> 9x (the cyclic
   group Z17 and the subgroup of order 8 of the multiplicative group of GF(17)) has order 136 and acts on these
   columns. Column 17 is fixed by G.

2. The rows. A word is a row of 18 entries. The rows of the array are the images under G of 7 words, with
   stabilizers of orders 2, 2, 4, 4, 8, 8 and 8, so orbits of 68, 68, 34, 34, 17, 17 and 17 rows (255), plus 4
   constant rows. A word with stabilizer H must be constant on the orbits of H, which leaves about 40 free bits in
   all.

3. The model. The covering condition is invariant under G, so it is enough to ask it for one (6-set, pattern) pair
   from each orbit. programs/eager.py writes one clause per orbit: some row of the array shows the pattern on the
   6-set, each candidate row a conjunction of free bits encoded with an auxiliary variable. Symmetry breaking fixes
   complements of columns and orders equal-stabilizer words lexicographically. CaDiCaL 1.5.3 (python-sat
   1.9.dev22) found a solution in 23 s; the program writes the 259 rows.

The array was checked exactly with ca_verify on all 18,564 sets of six columns.

Built from: nothing; the construction starts from the group and the SAT model. No array of Torres-Jimenez is an
ingredient or start.

Program: programs/recipe.sh runs programs/eager.py with the arguments 8 100000 2 2 4 4 8 8 8 (the order of the
multiplier subgroup, the conflict budget and the stabilizer orders) and converts its output to one row per line
with spaces. It is deterministic. A complete run from scratch reproduced the array exactly
(programs/recipe_log.txt). Needs python 3.13 and python-sat 1.9.dev22.
